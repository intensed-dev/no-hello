nohello

CHAT ETIQUETTE, SIMPLIFIED

Don't just say hello.

If you have a question, include it in your first message. Give people something they can actually respond to.

The problem with “Hello”

You open a chat, type “Hello” and send it. Then you wait for the other person to reply before explaining why you've got in touch.

That adds an unnecessary step. The other person has to stop what they're doing, open the conversation and reply — only to discover that the actual message is still on its way. Then you have to wait again for an answer to your question. This back-and-forth makes the whole conversation take longer than necessary.

A greeting is fine.

The problem is sending a greeting on its own when you already know what you want to ask.

Give the context straight away

Rather than splitting one conversation across several messages, send the greeting, context and question together. The recipient can read everything and reply when they're free.

THE SLOW WAY

YouHello!
AlexHi, what's up?
YouI wanted to ask about the project...

Three messages just to get to the question — with an extra wait in between.

THE BETTER WAY

YouHi Alex! I'm working on the project and have a question: which version should I use?

One message. All the context. No need to wait before getting an answer.

What to include

  • A greeting — start in a friendly, natural way.
  • Some context — briefly explain what you're talking about.
  • Your question or request — make it clear what you need.
For example

“Hi! I'm setting up the new website. Do you know which hosting provider we're using?”

And then?

Send your message and let the other person reply when they can. There's no need to ask whether they're there, send a separate “hello?”, or follow up straight away.

Everyone has a different schedule and may not be available immediately. A complete message lets them reply in their own time — without having to ask what you wanted first.
