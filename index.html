nohello

A tiny guide to better conversations

CHAT ETIQUETTE, SIMPLIFIED

Don't just say hello.

If you have a question, put it in your first message. Give people something they can actually respond to.

The problem with “Hello”

You open a chat, type “Hello” and send it. Then you wait for the other person to reply before you explain why you reached out.

That creates an unnecessary extra step. The other person has to stop what they're doing, open the conversation and reply — only to find out that the actual message is still coming.

A greeting is fine.

The problem is sending a greeting on its own when you already know what you want to ask.

Give the context right away

Instead of splitting one conversation into multiple messages, send the greeting, context and question together. The recipient can read everything and respond when they're available.

THE SLOW WAY

You Hello!
Alex Hey, what's up?
You I wanted to ask about the project...

Three messages just to get to the question.

THE BETTER WAY

You Hey Alex! I'm working on the project and had a question: which version should I use?

One message. All the context. Nothing to wait for.

What to include

  • A greeting — start in a friendly, natural way.
  • Some context — briefly explain what you're talking about.
  • Your question or request — make it clear what you need.
For example

“Hey! I'm setting up the new website. Do you know which hosting provider we're using?”

And then?

Send your message and let the other person reply when they can. You don't need to ask whether they're there, send a separate “hello?”, or follow up immediately.

People have different schedules and may not be available right away. A complete message lets them respond on their own time — without making them ask what you wanted first.
